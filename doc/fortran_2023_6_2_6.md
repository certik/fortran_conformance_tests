### S6.2.6-001: Delimiter tokens have seven listed spellings

**Source:** 6.2.6 p1; J3/24-007 PDF page 70, printed page 56, line 29. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** When a lexical token serves as a delimiter, its spelling comes from (, ), /, [, ], (/,
and /). The last two are the exact two-character delimiter spellings (/ and /). This
classification presupposes a delimiter token; it does not make every occurrence of one
of these characters a delimiter. The enclosing grammar decides the permitted context,
pairing, and contents.

**Diagnostic obligation:** context-dependent.

**Facets:** `left-parenthesis`, `right-parenthesis`, `slash`, `left-square-bracket`, `right-square-bracket`, `slash-array-open`, `slash-array-close`.

**Oracle:** Four separately compiled positive controls cover the seven spellings in legal contexts.
The parenthesis pair groups (left + right) before multiplication and checks 20 for
left=2 and right=3. Two single slash delimiters enclose an integer DATA value list whose
two separately checked results are 11 and 17. A square-bracket constructor supplies the
two values 5 and 9 to an explicitly shaped integer array. A separate (/ ... /)
constructor supplies 6 and 10. Each array element is checked independently; all values
and shapes are defined before use, and every program terminates without a loop.

**Oracle limitation:** These are admission controls, not full construct semantics or direct observations of
internal token boundaries. No malformed-pair or arbitrary-character rejection is
inferred from the list. In particular, / used for division, the operators // and /=, and
punctuation inside character constants or comments are not automatically delimiter
tokens. Equals, =>, comma, colon, ::, semicolon, .., ?, and % are separate 6.2.1
alternatives rather than additional members of this seven-item list. Parenthesis pairs
and each array-constructor pair occur together in their conforming controls; this is not
independent proof of opening/closing-token diagnostics. Detailed DATA, expression,
constructor, coarray, FORMAT, blank, and continuation rules remain with their defining
sections. A diagnostic claim would require the applicable enclosing syntax/constraint or
Clause 6 source-form/character obligation under 4.2, so none is manufactured here.

**Dependencies:** 6.2.1 supplies the lexical-token union. R1001 and 10.1.3 admit and interpret the
parenthesized integer expression (PDF 168, 172-173). 8.6.7 R840-R842/R848 and p8/p10
give the slash-delimited DATA context and one-to-one initialization of two same-type
scalar integers (PDF 136-138). 7.8 R777-R781/C7120 and p1/p2/p6 admit both constructor
spellings and specify the ordered rank-one values (PDF 114-115); R779 and R780 retain
canonical ownership of bracket syntax. 10.2.1.3 supplies assignment to the same-shape
integer arrays. 6.3.2.2-6.3.2.5 and 7.4.4.3 retain source-form and character-context
rules; 6.2.4 and Clause 10 retain operator syntax. 4.2 p2(1) supplies conforming
execution, while p2(3)/(5) concerns actual numbered-syntax/constraint or
source-form/character violations.

These ordinary valid programs do not expose the implementation's scanner or
prove token-boundary rules. Both members of each matched pair occur in one
conforming construct, not in artificial independent rejection cases.
Punctuation inside a literal or comment, a division slash, `//`, and `/=`
must not be counted as delimiters merely by character matching. The separate
`=` alternative in 6.2.1 is not a defined operator or an extra 6.2.6 delimiter.

Array-constructor, DATA, expression, coarray, FORMAT, and source-form
constraints stay with their defining sections. This draft claims admission
in the chosen contexts only. Source classification and the runtime oracles
still require independent review; neither compiler agreement nor a locally
registered index entry supplies that approval.
