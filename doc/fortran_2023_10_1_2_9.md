### S10.1.2.9-001: Defined binary operators occupy the lowest expression precedence layer

**Source:** 10.1.2.9 p1; J3/24-007 PDF p171. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** An expr is a level-5 expression optionally involving defined binary operators, and
defined binary operators have the lowest operator precedence. Thus intrinsic numeric,
character, relational, and logical layers bind inside the operands of defined binary
operations unless parentheses alter grouping.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `level-5-base`, `defined-binary-after-intrinsic`, `defined-binary-left-chain`, `parenthesized-defined-binary-operand`.

**Oracle:** Future precedence controls must use valid defined-operation interfaces and
literal-distinguishable results; generic dispatch and operation interpretation remain
dependencies.

### R1023: An expr is a level-5 expression optionally preceded by an expr and defined binary operator

**Source:** 10.1.2.9 R1023; J3/24-007 PDF p171. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** The expr syntax is a level-5-expr or an expr followed by defined-binary-op and
level-5-expr. This makes unparenthesized defined binary operations left-recursive and
lower precedence than all intrinsic operator levels.

**Diagnostic obligation:** required.

### R1024: A defined binary operator is a dotted sequence of letters

**Source:** 10.1.2.9 R1024; J3/24-007 PDF p171. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** The defined-binary-op spelling is a period, one or more letters, and a period. Digits,
underscores, missing dots, and empty names are not part of this syntactic form.

### C1006: Defined binary operator names are short and not intrinsic or logical-literal spellings

**Source:** 10.1.2.9 C1006; J3/24-007 PDF p171. **Class:** Restriction.

**Definition:** A defined-binary-op shall not contain more than 63 letters and shall not be the same as
