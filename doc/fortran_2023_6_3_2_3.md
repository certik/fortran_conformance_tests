### S6.3.2.3-001: Free-form comments leave program interpretation unchanged

**Source:** 6.3.2.3 p1; J3/24-007, 18 December 2023, PDF page 72, printed page 58, lines 2-6. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** Outside the two character contexts, an exclamation point starts commentary covering the
remainder of its physical line. A line whose first nonblank character is an exclamation
point, an all-blank line, and a zero-character line are comment lines. Commentary may
occur inside a program unit or on either side of its statements without changing
interpretation. Within a character literal or a character string edit descriptor, an
exclamation point instead belongs to that character context.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `inline-comment`, `bang-line`, `blank-line`, `empty-line`, `inside-unit`, `before-unit`, `after-unit`, `literal-context`, `edit-descriptor-context`, `consecutive-lines`.

**Oracle:** One program checks initialized scalar and character sentinels while trailing commentary
contains misleading assignment, quote, semicolon, and ampersand spellings. Literal and
labeled FORMAT contexts retain embedded exclamation points in exact character results;
internal output is checked only after IOSTAT succeeds. Position-specific IACHAR results
of 33 independently check those exclamation points, so a source literal and its
comparand cannot pass merely by losing the same character. A byte-preserved fixture
distinguishes empty lines from lines of three blanks, places comments before, inside,
and after a unit, and includes a finite run of 300 comment lines. It checks the
unchanged sentinel immediately after the comments, before overwriting it.

**Oracle limitation:** The 300-line witness is finite evidence, not proof of an unbounded capacity or a new
limit derived from the note. Character repertoire and source-form selection remain
processor-qualified dependencies; these inputs use only ordinary letters, digits,
blanks, and the needed mandatory punctuation, with no additional graphics or control
characters. No requirement to diagnose commentary is invented. FORMAT, character literal
spelling, and the semantics of the enclosing assignments are not owned here.

**Dependencies:** 3.21 (PDF 21) supplies both character contexts. 3.96 and 6.3.1 p1-p3 (PDF 31/71) supply
physical lines, composition, character repertoire permission, and processor-selected
source form. 6.1.1/6.1.5/6.1.6 retain character-set ownership. 7.4.4.3, 13.2.1, and 13.9
(PDF 85-86/289/307) supply the character and output contexts. 9.4.1 and 16.9.98 (PDF
151/416) justify in-bounds one-character substrings and ASCII inquiries; IACHAR does not
assume native ASCII encoding. 4.2 p2(1), PDF 46, qualifies conforming execution by
processor size and complexity limits.
