### S10.1.5.2.1-001: Mixed numeric intrinsic binary operands are interpreted through result-type conversion with the integer-power exception

**Source:** 10.1.5.2.1 p1; J3/24-007 PDF p176. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** The two operands of a numeric intrinsic binary operation may differ in numeric type or
kind type parameter. Except for real or complex raised to an integer power, each operand
whose type or kind differs from the result is interpreted as if converted to the result
type and kind before the operation is performed. For real or complex raised to an
integer power, the integer operand is not required to be converted; that is latitude,
not an observable obligation.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `mixed-type-as-if-conversion`, `mixed-kind-as-if-conversion`, `real-base-integer-power-exception`, `complex-base-integer-power-exception`.

**Oracle:** Use type/kind inquiries and exact small integer, real, or complex values whose
mathematical result is not altered by permitted reassociation or other 10.1.5.2.4
latitude. Treat the integer-power sentence as a permission boundary: no negative or
positive may infer the processor's internal conversion choice.

### S10.1.5.2.1-002: Numeric operations express numeric computations and produce numeric values

**Source:** 10.1.5.2.1 p2; J3/24-007 PDF p176. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** A numeric operation expresses a numeric computation and evaluating it produces a numeric
value. The permitted operand data types are those specified by 10.1.5.1, not by this
subclause independently.

### S10.1.5.2.1-003: Table 10.3 gives the interpretations of the numeric intrinsic operators

**Source:** 10.1.5.2.1 p3 and Table 10.3; J3/24-007 PDF p176. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** For a numeric intrinsic expression, Table 10.3 interprets ** as raising x1 to the power
x2, / as dividing x1 by x2, * as multiplying x1 by x2, binary - as subtracting x2 from
x1, unary - as negating x2, binary + as adding x1 and x2, and unary + as the identity
operation on x2. In the table, x1 is the left operand and x2 the right operand. The
table extraction was checked row by row; no column ambiguity remains for these rows.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `exponentiation-operator`, `division-operator`, `multiplication-operator`, `subtraction-operator`, `negation-operator`, `addition-operator`, `identity-operator`.

**Oracle:** Use one-operation expressions where the table row, not precedence or mixed-mode
conversion, is the attribution target. Table 10.3 row/column extraction has a
straightforward one-row mapping and no unresolved table ambiguity.

### S10.1.5.2.1-004: Integer exponentiation with a negative integer exponent is interpreted through reciprocal integer division

**Source:** 10.1.5.2.1 p5; J3/24-007 PDF p176. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** If x1 and x2 are both integer and x2 is negative, x1 ** x2 has the same interpretation
as 1/(x1 ** ABS(x2)), subject to the integer division rules of 10.1.5.2.2.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `positive-base-negative-exponent`, `negative-base-negative-exponent`, `abs-exponent-dependency`.

**Oracle:** Use nonzero integer bases and small negative exponents so exponentiation and the
following integer division are both defined. Do not generalize to real or complex
negative exponents, and do not use division by zero or overflow as evidence.
