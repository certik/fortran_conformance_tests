## Semantic and oracle boundaries

Every real method must be available for both complex components with the
same kind. Default complex has default-real kind, not a prescribed storage
format. The KIND parameter's default-integer type remains S7.4.1-001.

With two real parts, greater decimal precision chooses the literal kind.
Equal precision permits the processor to choose either part's kind,
including when their ranges differ. It does not import
SELECTED_REAL_KIND's smallest-kind tie rule or require the same choice
after reversing parts. Nonmatching parts are converted to the chosen
method. Two integer parts give default complex; one integer part adopts
the other, real part's method. A receiving object's kind is not a literal
kind oracle.

Unprofiled zero-payload cases can test kind selection without inventing
conversion accuracy. Nonzero component/order and conversion witnesses
retain independent finite-value/approximation premises. No bit layout,
IEEE encoding, arbitrary tolerance, narrowing of kind identifiers or
relationship between integer and real kind numbers is assumed.

The pending complete-inventory witness has at most sixteen real methods.
Equal-precision/equal-range and equal-precision/unequal-range pairs are
separate optional profile branches. No such pair means no observation of
that branch, not proof that ties are forbidden.

## Diagnostic boundary

Complex literal parts are restricted syntax, not arbitrary constant
expressions. Signs are permitted on literal parts, not as a general prefix
to named constants. Each named part must independently be scalar and of
integer or real type.

The authored pairs check both positions for a constant arithmetic
expression, a signed name, rank and type. Integer-literal, real-literal and
named-part controls are separate so missing named-part support cannot
mask literal alternatives. The expression pair uses zero real literals,
not a name that a processor might reject even without the extra operator.
A rank repair substitutes a scalar named constant, not an array element
that would violate the part grammar. Genuine variable-name constancy cases
remain pending with canonical R606 ownership and a separately reviewed
use-context connection.

Causes and exact part-use points are qualified independently:
generic type/constant/exponent words cannot pass, and a constancy complaint
about an already-constant expression is not the intended syntax report.
Unsupported facilities, crashes, verifier and resource failures are not
reporting evidence. Numbered diagnostics need not be fatal; no additional
prose-rejection policy is introduced.

The missing/extra separator and forbidden-operator contracts retain only
their existing token-specific routes. Bare comma, closing-parenthesis and
decimal-point expectations are not accepted: the matcher cannot enforce
the previously claimed compiler-family or literal-context relationship.
This remains true when an actual Flang report has the appropriate context
chain; those terminal reports are now unconfirmed raw observations rather
than passing contract evidence. No new context-matching subsystem or
counterexample-word blacklist is introduced.

The signed-name contracts now use explicitly prospective text identifying
the forbidden sign/name relationship in the real or imaginary part.
Their unchanged sources and minimal repairs remain source-justified, but
no observed compiler support for that prospective text is claimed. They
remain source-only candidates for independent fixture adjudication.

GNU's numeric-type and scalar-rank reports are distinguished from its separate
PARAMETER-lookup failure; the numeric-type report cannot qualify a complex
part that is already numeric. Flang's type message lists implementation
extensions, but no UNSIGNED or BOZ source is used or credited. Its exact
part-not-scalar portability messages can satisfy reporting without fatal
rejection. Unexpected-name errors that also reject repaired named controls
are implementation limitations, not C723 evidence.

Nonzero component identity/conversion, equal-precision tie profiles and
complete method inventories remain pending. The cases stay in their
declared run or compile phases when implementations lack support, and
Flang f2018 acceptance is not relabeled as f2023 corroboration.

### R718: A complex literal is a parenthesized pair separated by a comma

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 R718, PDF 83, printed 69, line 12. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** A complex literal encloses a real-part and an imag-part in parentheses with one
separating comma. The first and second part positions have their own productions, R719
and R720. Token-separating blanks are permitted by the canonical source-form rules, not
by treating the whole pair as one real token.

**Diagnostic obligation:** required.

**Facets:** `parenthesized-pair`, `spaced-pair`, `comma-required`, `exactly-two-parts`.

**Oracle:** The unchanged controls compile complete same-kind zero-valued DATA initializations. Each
negative changes one separator/arity feature on one source line. Only the existing
token-specific reports identifying the unexpected second real token or extra comma are
retained. Unconstrained expected-comma and expected-parenthesis alternatives are
removed.

**Oracle limitation:** A one-item parenthesized real expression is not an isolated R718 negative in ordinary
assignment, where numeric conversion may make it conforming. A file/line match and a
minimal repair do not make a bare terminal message context-specific. The matcher does
not bind compiler family or diagnostic context chains for these routes; even a genuine
Flang COMPLEX-context terminal report remains an unconfirmed raw observation rather than
passing contract evidence. Generic complex/type/constant errors, unrelated parenthesis
recovery, unsupported facilities, crashes, verifier and resource failures remain
excluded.

**Dependencies:** R719/R720/C723; 6.3.2.2 p1, PDF 71; 8.6.7/R848 and p8/p10, PDF 137-138; 4.2 p2(3), PDF
46. Component values and order are separately S7.4.3.3-001.

### R719: The real part admits signed numeric literals or a named constant

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 R719, PDF 83, printed 69, lines 13-15. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** The first part of a complex literal is a signed integer literal, a signed real literal,
or a named constant. The named alternative is subject to C723. An arbitrary constant
expression, a variable, or a sign applied to a name is not an additional alternative
merely because an ordinary expression could use it.

**Diagnostic obligation:** required.

**Facets:** `signed-integer-part`, `signed-real-part`, `named-part`, `expression-not-part`, `variable-not-named-constant`, `sign-before-name`.

**Oracle:** The independent first-position integer-literal, real-literal and named-part controls are
unchanged. The arithmetic-expression pair retains 0.0D0+0.0D0 versus 0.0D0 and only the
existing unexpected-plus token route. The signed-name pair retains -r versus the same
already constant r. Its prospective cause contract explicitly identifies a forbidden
sign before a named constant in the real part of a complex literal, as required by
R719's alternatives; no observed compiler support for that text is claimed. The
variable-name constancy route remains pending with canonical R606 ownership.

**Oracle limitation:** The rule permits signed literals, not general signed constant expressions. Constancy
complaints about the already-constant expression or -r, failed PARAMETER/name lookup,
and missing named-part implementation support remain wrong causes. Bare comma/dot
expectations are not accepted, even when the raw output includes genuine literal-context
labels: the matcher cannot enforce the previously claimed relationship. The signed-name
diagnostic remains a source-only/prospective-contract candidate for independent
adjudication. C723's scalar/type negatives remain separate, and no source or repaired
control is changed to force an observation.

**Dependencies:** R707/R708/R713/R714 and R606; 8.5.13 p1, PDF 130, defines PARAMETER; 10.1.12 p1, PDF
187-188, distinguishes constant expressions from the narrower part syntax. 4.1.2 p2 and
4.2 p2(3), PDF 45-46.

### R720: The imaginary part independently admits signed numeric literals or a named constant

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 R720, PDF 83, printed 69, lines 16-18. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** The second part of a complex literal has the signed integer literal, signed real literal
and named-constant alternatives. The restriction is independently applied in the
imaginary position. C723 supplies the named constant's scalar and integer-or-real
conditions.

**Diagnostic obligation:** required.

**Facets:** `signed-integer-part`, `signed-real-part`, `named-part`, `expression-not-part`, `variable-not-named-constant`, `sign-before-name`.

**Oracle:** The independent second-position controls and the two source/repair pairs are unchanged.
The 0.0D0+0.0D0 pair retains only the existing unexpected-plus token route. The -r pair
has a prospective cause contract explicitly identifying a forbidden sign before a named
constant in the imaginary part of a complex literal, following R720's alternatives; that
text has no claimed observed compiler support. Unexpected-name errors that also reject
valid named controls do not qualify. The variable-name constancy pair remains pending
with R606.

**Oracle limitation:** R719's executions are not assumed to cover R720, and descendant sign/suffix/digit owners
are unchanged. Bare closing-parenthesis or decimal-point messages no longer qualify; a
source-line match cannot enforce their missing literal-context relationship. The actual
Flang context chain is retained only as an unconfirmed raw observation. The signed-name
contract remains source-only/prospective pending independent adjudication; no
family/context-matching subsystem or counterexample-word blacklist is introduced.
Constancy, PARAMETER, implementation, crash, verifier and resource exclusions and the
distinction between reporting and fatal rejection are preserved.

**Dependencies:** R707/R708/R713/R714, R606 and C723; 8.5.13 and 10.1.12, PDF 130 and 187-188; 4.2 p2(3),

### C723: Every named complex-literal part is scalar and integer or real

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 C723 (R718), PDF 83, printed 69, line 19. **Class:** Restriction.

**Definition:** Each named constant used in either part of a complex literal is scalar and has integer
or real type. Both properties apply separately to each named part. A scalar constant of
another type fails the type condition; an integer or real array constant fails the
scalar condition even if it has only one element.

**Diagnostic obligation:** required.

**Facets:** `scalar-integer-names`, `scalar-real-names`, `real-part-rank`, `imaginary-part-rank`, `real-part-type`, `imaginary-part-type`.

**Oracle:** Two supported controls and eight negative/control pairs cover both named-part positions,
scalar rank and logical/character/complex excluded-type representatives. Rank repair
substitutes a scalar name, never an array element. GNU's Scalar PARAMETER route checks
rank after constant lookup, while its Numeric PARAMETER route checks numeric type and
qualifies only the logical/character pairs; complex is already numeric and that route
cannot qualify its exclusion. Flang's operand-type whitelist reports qualify these
excluded types, and its exact real/imaginary-part-not-scalar portability messages
qualify rank nonfatally.

**Oracle limitation:** These are finite named-part samples, not every derived/enum/enumeration type. The
upstream GNU 16.1 diagnostic paths were checked separately from Fortran source validity:
its PARAMETER-lookup failure is a different, excluded cause. Flang's observed type
message also lists implementation extensions; no UNSIGNED or BOZ source is admitted or
credited here. The target's unexpected-identifier reports also reject the conforming
named controls and cannot stand in for C723. Generic parser/type/constant words, false
constancy complaints, facilities, crashes, verifier and resource failures remain
excluded. Current reference-mode and silent-acceptance limitations require independent
fixture adjudication, not self-approval.

**Dependencies:** R718-R720 and R606; 8.5.13 p1, PDF 130; R606 and 8.6.7/R848, PDF 137, distinguish a
named constant from a constant subobject without adding a part-grammar alternative.
4.1.2 p2 and 4.2 p2(3), PDF 45-46, apply this numbered constraint wherever the complex
literal occurs.

### S7.4.3.3-001: Complex values have ordered real and imaginary components

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 p1, PDF 83, printed 69, lines 2-4. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** A complex value is an ordered pair of real values approximating a mathematical complex
number. Its first component is the real part and its second is the imaginary part. These
names and order describe values, not interleaved memory, storage-unit counts or a pair
of prescribed floating-point bit patterns.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `real-part-first`, `imaginary-part-second`, `component-value-identity`.

**Oracle:** Component designators compare the actual already-defined input real values rather than
reconstructing an expected complex value with CMPLX, which might share the same defect.
The first and second values are independently checked.

**Oracle limitation:** The finite distinct-value premise must be justified, not inferred from arbitrary literal
accuracy or a bit cast. The pair contract itself does not promise an IEEE
representation, layout or exact conversion between unlike real methods. Component
inquiries are dependent mechanisms and remain runtime effects if an implementation lacks
them.

**Dependencies:** 9.4.4/R915 and p1, PDF 153, supplies real component designators with the complex kind;
16.9.10 AIMAG, PDF 378-379. 16.9.172 REAL, PDF 449-450, has an approximation contract,
so it is not used as an unqualified exact-layout oracle.

### S7.4.3.3-002: Every real method is available for both components of complex with the same kind

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 p2, PDF 83, printed 69, lines 5-8, excluding the separately linked default-integer parameter-type restatement. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** Every real approximation method is available for both components of complex. A complex
kind value selects that same real method for each part, and KIND returns that value for
the complex entity. A component does not independently choose another kind. The KIND
parameter's own default-integer type is the existing 7.4.1 requirement.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `default-real-correspondence`, `double-real-correspondence`, `real-component-kind`, `imaginary-component-kind`, `kind-inquiry`, `finite-all-method-correspondence`.

**Oracle:** The unprofiled default/double cases and a complete bounded inventory directly exercise
the real-to-complex kind correspondence. Optional selected kinds may alias default,
double or each other; each distinct inventory entry is considered without ordering
assumptions.

**Oracle limitation:** REAL_KINDS is used because the source requires corresponding real methods; no separate
complex-storage inventory is invented. The finite inventory is not proof for more than
sixteen methods or a physical representation/value-set specification. The parameter
named KIND and its default-integer type keep S7.4.1-001 ownership.

**Dependencies:** 7.4.1 p2/S7.4.1-001, PDF 80; 7.4.3.2 p1/p5/p6; 9.4.4, PDF 153; 16.9.10/118/172, PDF
378-379, 424 and 449-450; 16.10.2.26, PDF 476.

### S7.4.3.3-003: Omitted COMPLEX selection gives default-real kind for both parts

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 p3, PDF 83, printed 69, lines 9-11; default semantics only. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** COMPLEX without a kind parameter denotes default complex. Its kind value equals
KIND(0.0), and each component is default real. No special double-precision complex
keyword is defined; an explicit complex kind selector supplies that method through the
canonical grammar.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `default-complex-kind`, `default-real-component`, `default-imaginary-component`.

**Oracle:** The selector-free declaration and both actual component types/kinds supply exact runtime
inquiry/type-agreement oracles. Zero payloads avoid a mixed-method value-conversion
premise.

**Oracle limitation:** R704/R706/C717 retain declaration syntax and support constraints. No DOUBLE COMPLEX
extension negative is manufactured here. CMPLX has its own default-kind rule and is not
substituted for a plain COMPLEX declaration.

**Dependencies:** R704/R706/C717, PDF 80; 9.4.4, PDF 153; 16.9.118, PDF 424. 16.9.53 CMPLX, PDF 394-395,
remains a separate intrinsic owner.

### S7.4.3.3-004: Two real parts select by decimal precision, with processor choice on ties and conversion if needed

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 p4, PDF 83, printed 69, lines 20-24. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** When both parts of a complex literal are real, the literal takes the kind of the part
with greater decimal precision. If their decimal precisions tie, the processor chooses
the kind of one of those parts. A part with a different kind is converted to the chosen
complex approximation method. Range, radix, storage size and numerical kind order are
not additional tie-breakers in this paragraph.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `greater-precision-first`, `greater-precision-second`, `same-kind-parts`, `distinct-equal-precision-equal-range`, `distinct-equal-precision-different-range`, `first-part-conversion`, `second-part-conversion`.

**Oracle:** Mandatory unequal-precision and same-kind controls use literal KIND inquiries and zero
components. Tie cases accept the explicitly permitted finite set of two kind values,
then require both components to have the selected kind. Value-conversion cases need
separate, independently justified rational/processor expectations.

**Oracle limitation:** The tie choice need not be invariant under operand order, equal range or another
selected-kind call. Do not borrow SELECTED_REAL_KIND's smallest-kind tie rule. A larger
decimal precision need not imply larger exponent range, so arbitrary extreme values
cannot test conversion safely. Unknown conversion accuracy is a genuine pending oracle
gap, not a reason to require nearest rounding, add a tolerance or downgrade the run
phase.

**Dependencies:** 7.4.3.2 p5/p6, PDF 82; 16.9.162/170, PDF 445 and 449; 16.10.2.26, PDF 476; 9.4.4, PDF
153. 16.9.172 REAL and 16.9.53 CMPLX, PDF 449-450 and 394-395, are dependencies with
processor-dependent conversion, not alternate complex-literal selection rules.

### S7.4.3.3-005: Integer parts convert to default real or to the method of the real-typed part

**Source:** 7.4.3.3 p5, PDF 83, printed 69, lines 25-28. **Class:** Effect.

**Definition:** If both parts of a complex literal are integers, both are converted to default real and
the result is default complex. If exactly one part is integer, it is converted to the
method of the part whose type is real, and that real-typed part's kind is the literal's
kind, regardless of its position. Integer kind identifiers, integer range and the
receiving variable do not select the literal's complex kind.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `both-default-integers`, `both-selected-integers`, `integer-first-real-second`, `real-first-integer-second`, `both-integer-value-conversion`, `mixed-integer-value-conversion`.

**Oracle:** Unprofiled zero-payload effects directly check all kind-selection branches without
narrowing any identifier or relying on real conversion accuracy. Separately profiled
nonzero payloads test the actual component conversions in a bounded family.

**Oracle limitation:** Do not promote the complex kind because an integer part uses a larger-range integer
method, assume integer and real kinds share code meanings, or use an arbitrary kind
number as a real payload. In a both-integer literal, a double-kind complex receiving
object does not change the literal's default-complex kind. Nonzero conversion oracles
remain pending until their approximation premises are independently established.

**Dependencies:** 7.4.3.1 p2 and the reviewed integer catalogue supply the supported range-18 method;
16.9.181, PDF 454-455. 7.4.3.2 p5/p6, PDF 82; 16.9.118, PDF 424; 9.4.4, PDF 153; numeric
assignment and REAL conversion, PDF 191 and 449-450.
