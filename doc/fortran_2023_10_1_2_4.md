### S10.1.2.4-001: Defined unary operators occupy the highest precedence layer

**Source:** 10.1.2.4 p1; J3/24-007 PDF p169. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** Level-1 expressions are primaries optionally operated on by defined unary operators, and
defined unary operators have the highest operator precedence. This precedence statement
is structural; defined-operation applicability and interpretation remain dependencies.

### R1003: A level-1 expression is an optional defined unary operator followed by a primary

**Source:** 10.1.2.4 R1003; J3/24-007 PDF p169. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** The level-1-expr syntax is either a primary or a defined-unary-op immediately followed
by a primary. The operator is prefix and non-repeating in this grammar level unless
another primary form introduces parentheses or a lower expression.

**Diagnostic obligation:** required.

**Facets:** `primary-only`, `defined-unary-primary`, `two-unary-without-parentheses-rejected`.

**Oracle:** Negatives must use well-formed defined-unary-op spellings and valid interfaces so the

### R1004: A defined unary operator is a dotted sequence of letters

**Source:** 10.1.2.4 R1004; J3/24-007 PDF p169. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** The defined-unary-op spelling is a period, one or more letters, and a period. Digits,

### C1005: Defined unary operator names are short and not intrinsic or logical-literal spellings

**Source:** 10.1.2.4 C1005; J3/24-007 PDF p169. **Class:** Restriction.

**Definition:** A defined-unary-op shall not contain more than 63 letters and shall not be the same as
