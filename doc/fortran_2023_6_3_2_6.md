### S6.3.2.6-001: A free-form whole statement may carry a preceding label

**Source:** 6.3.2.6 p1; J3/24-007 PDF page 72, printed page 58, line 29. **Class:** Syntax.

**Definition:** A label is permitted before a statement that is not embedded as part of another
statement. In free form this includes a whole statement following a semicolon and a
statement spread over several continued physical lines. The permission does not attach a
second label to an embedded action statement or waive the canonical label spelling,
uniqueness, or permitted-use rules.

**Diagnostic obligation:** not-required.

**Facets:** `whole-statements`, `after-semicolon`, `continued-statement`, `enclosing-if`.

**Oracle:** One positive control labels a declaration, assignments, an enclosing action-IF
statement, and END PROGRAM, including a labeled assignment continued across lines. A
separate control branches forward to an assignment after a semicolon, so lack of that
form does not mask the other label contexts. Defined scalar checks provide finite
executable context; they are not full effects of the label permission.

**Oracle limitation:** The embedded-statement condition is source-accounted, not turned into a negative under
this permission. R1141 and the canonical label owner govern the nested action syntax;
this batch neither reallocates those R/C IDs nor claims coverage of all labeled
statement classes. The informative digit observation does not need a manufactured
executable effect.

**Dependencies:** 3.130 and 6.3.1 define statements. 6.2.5/R611/C603 retain label spelling and use
ownership (PDF 70). 6.3.2.4 and 6.3.2.5 supply free-form layout. R1141/11.1.8.4 (PDF
219) distinguishes the enclosing IF from its action. 4.2 p2(1) supplies syntax admission
subject to processor resource qualifications.

### S6.3.2.6-002: A free-form statement contains at most one million source characters

**Source:** 6.3.2.6 p2; J3/24-007 PDF page 72, printed page 58, line 30. **Class:** Restriction.

**Definition:** The total source-character length of one free-form statement cannot exceed 1000000. This
is neither a file-byte limit nor a limit on the resulting value of a character literal.
Continuation markers and semicolon terminators are excluded by their defining
subclauses; interposed commentary and physical line separators are not statement text.
Literal quote delimiters remain part of the statement's source spelling even though they
do not belong to the literal value.

**Diagnostic obligation:** required.

**Facets:** `at-limit`, `one-over-limit`, `continuation-exclusion`, `comment-exclusion`, `terminator-exclusion`.

**Oracle:** A deterministic generator emits two byte-preserved assignments of exactly 1000000 and
1000001 source characters. Their only byte difference is one inserted x before the
terminal Z of the literal. Both use 250 noncomment source lines, hence 249 continuation
lines, with a maximum physical line length of 4004; neither violates the F2023
physical-line rule. Twelve interposed comment lines and a semicolon terminator are
excluded. The at-limit control checks every literal character, terminal Z, and one blank
of assignment padding. The over-limit compile-only diagnose oracle requires a
statement-length-specific message within the reconstructed assignment's declared source
span, rather than insisting on only its final line.

**Oracle limitation:** The explicit kind SELECTED_INT_KIND(9) is justified by 7.4.3.1 p2: the processor must
supply an integer representation with decimal range at least 18. All large length
literals, loop bounds, substring indices, and LEN inquiries use the selected kind; the
weaker default-integer minimum of range 5 is not enough for the boundary. No optional
profile gates this mandatory premise. Positive admission remains subject to the 4.2
p2(1) size/complexity qualification; an observed resource failure is not silently
rewritten as a fixture defect. Flang's f2018 fallback cannot independently validate the
F2023 length premise. An unrelated source location, whole-program recovery range,
physical-line/legacy-continuation limit, character-capacity error, crash or timeout
cannot replace the statement-length report. Numeric limit text alone is not an accepted
message predicate. No permissive compiler option or broad warning allowance is added.

**Dependencies:** 3.96/3.130 and 6.3.1 define physical lines and statements. 6.3.2.3 excludes commentary,
6.3.2.4 p1 excludes continuation markers, and 6.3.2.5 p2 excludes semicolon terminators.
7.4.4.3/R724 distinguishes source spelling from literal value. 7.4.3.1 p2/p4 (PDF 80)

## Exact-boundary regeneration

Run `python3 tools/generate_free_source_boundaries.py`, or use `--check` to
compare without writing. The two raw fixtures differ by one inserted `x`.
Their assignments have no inter-token whitespace or doubled quote delimiters:
the logical spelling is `s='` plus the payload plus the closing quote.
Thus four source characters are added to each payload's length. The payloads
have 999996 and 999997 characters, giving 1000000 and 1000001 respectively.

The 249 trailing and 249 leading continuation markers, twelve complete comment
lines, physical line separators, and final semicolon are excluded. The quotes
are included. Every physical line stays below 10000 characters. Both cases
use `SELECTED_INT_KIND(9)` for large values. Its availability follows from
7.4.3.1 p2's mandatory integer representation with range at least 18; default
integer need only have range 5. No optional profile, default-range assumption,
or unallocated storage is involved. Source review must distinguish this admission control from the
separate over-limit reporting effect and from processor resource limits.
